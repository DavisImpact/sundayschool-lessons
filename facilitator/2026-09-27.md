    <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold mb-4 text-[#1e3a5f]">Opening</h2>

    <div class="bg-white border border-slate-200 rounded-2xl p-7 mb-6">
      <p class="font-medium mb-3">Read the introduction paragraph from the manual:</p>
      <div class="bg-slate-50 border-l-4 border-[#1e3a5f] p-5 text-[15px] italic leading-relaxed">
        One thing that the Lord asks prophets to do is to warn about the consequences of sin. For Old Testament prophets, this often meant telling the rulers of mighty kingdoms that they needed to repent. It was a dangerous task, but Isaiah was fearless, and his warnings to the kingdoms of his day—including Israel, Judah, and surrounding nations—were bold (see Isaiah 13–23).
        <br><br>
        However, Isaiah also had a message of hope. Even though the prophesied destructions did come upon these kingdoms, Isaiah foresaw a chance for restoration and renewal. The Lord would invite His people to return to Him. He would make “the parched ground … become a pool, and the thirsty land springs of water” (Isaiah 35:7). He would perform “a marvellous work and a wonder” (Isaiah 29:14), restoring to Israel the blessings He had promised them. Neither Isaiah nor anyone else alive at that time lived to see this marvelous work. But we are seeing it today. In fact, we are part of it.
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="bg-white border border-slate-200 rounded-2xl p-7">

      </div>
      <p class="text-sm text-slate-500 mt-3">Celebrate different approaches: audio, family study, marking one phrase, followHIM, Gospel Library videos, or reading with a child.</p>

  <!-- Section 1 -->
  <div class="mb-12">
    <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold mb-2">1. Pride and worldliness will fail</h2>
    <p class="text-sm text-slate-500 mb-4">Isaiah 13–14</p>

    <div class="bg-white border border-slate-200 rounded-2xl p-7">
      <p class="text-[15.5px] mb-4 text-slate-700">Babylon was a real empire. Isaiah also uses it as a symbol for pride, greed, and sin that still surround us. Isaiah 14:12–15 pairs the fall of Babylon’s king with the fall of Lucifer.</p>
      <p class="font-semibold mb-3">Verses that invite conversation</p>
      <ul class="space-y-2.5 text-[15.5px] mb-6">
        <li>• Isaiah 13:11 — “I will cause the arrogancy of the proud to cease.”</li>
        <li>• Isaiah 14:3 — the Lord gives “rest from thy sorrow, and from thy fear.”</li>
        <li>• Isaiah 14:13–14 — “I will ascend… I will be like the most High.”</li>
      </ul>
      <p class="font-semibold mb-3">Discussion Prompts</p>
      <ul class="space-y-2.5 text-[15.5px]">
        <li>• What stood out as you read about Babylon or Lucifer?</li>
        <li>• Where do you notice Babylon’s pull toward power, status, or pleasure in ordinary life?</li>
        <li>• How has the Savior given you rest from sorrow or fear?</li>
      </ul>
      <div class="mt-6">
        <p class="font-semibold mb-2">Application Question</p>
        <div class="bg-yellow-50 border-l-4 border-yellow-400 p-5 rounded-xl">
          What warning from these chapters feels personal this week—and what invitation do you hear from the Lord instead?
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <!-- Section 2 -->
  <div class="mb-12">
    <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold mb-2">2. Jesus Christ is the promised Messiah</h2>
    <p class="text-sm text-slate-500 mb-4">Isaiah 22:22–23; 24:21–23; 25:6–8; 26:19; 28:16</p>

    <div class="bg-white border border-slate-200 rounded-2xl p-7">
      <p class="text-[15.5px] mb-4 text-slate-700">You do not need to cover every verse. Invite people to share the one image that stayed with them.</p>
      <p class="font-semibold mb-3">Key images</p>
      <ul class="space-y-2.5 text-[15.5px] mb-6">
        <li>• Isaiah 22:22 — the key of the house of David on His shoulder</li>
        <li>• Isaiah 25:8 — He will swallow up death in victory and wipe away tears</li>
        <li>• Isaiah 26:19 — “Thy dead men shall live.”</li>
        <li>• Isaiah 28:16 — a precious cornerstone, a sure foundation in Zion</li>
      </ul>
      <p class="font-semibold mb-3">Discussion Prompts</p>
      <ul class="space-y-2.5 text-[15.5px]">
        <li>• Which of these images of Christ felt most real to you this week? Why?</li>
        <li>• When have you needed a sure foundation rather than something that looked impressive?</li>
        <li>• What does “swallow up death in victory” mean in a home that has known loss?</li>
      </ul>
      <div class="mt-6">
        <p class="font-semibold mb-2">Application Question</p>
        <div class="bg-yellow-50 border-l-4 border-yellow-400 p-5 rounded-xl">
          What did you find in the scriptures this week that taught you something about the Savior?
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <!-- Section 3 -->
  <div class="mb-12">
    <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold mb-2">3. Turning away from the Lord brings spiritual danger</h2>
    <p class="text-sm text-slate-500 mb-4">Isaiah 24; 29:13; 30:8–14</p>

    <div class="bg-white border border-slate-200 rounded-2xl p-7">
      <p class="text-[15.5px] mb-4 text-slate-700">Isaiah names warning signs: changing ordinances, drawing near with lips while hearts are far, and saying to the seers, “See not.” He also uses comparisons—an empty earth, drunkenness, hunger, a wall ready to fall.</p>
      <p class="font-semibold mb-3">Discussion Prompts</p>
      <ul class="space-y-2.5 text-[15.5px]">
        <li>• Which warning sign or comparison caught your attention?</li>
        <li>• What does it look like, in real life, to honor the Lord with our lips while our hearts drift?</li>
        <li>• Why is it important to you to stay close to the Lord right now?</li>
      </ul>
      <div class="mt-6">
        <p class="font-semibold mb-2">Application Question</p>
        <div class="bg-yellow-50 border-l-4 border-yellow-400 p-5 rounded-xl">
          If the Spirit pointed out a place where you have been drifting, what is one small way to turn back this week?
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <!-- Section 4 -->
  <div class="mb-12">
    <h2 class="text-2xl font-semibold mb-2">4. The Lord can restore what is lost or broken</h2>
    <p class="text-sm text-slate-500 mb-4">Isaiah 29; 30:18–26; 35</p>

    <div class="bg-white border border-slate-200 rounded-2xl p-7">
      <p class="text-[15.5px] mb-4 text-slate-700">This is the heart of the week. Isaiah 29 is the Restoration—the sealed book, the unlearned reading it, “a marvellous work and a wonder.” Isaiah 35 is personal healing: the desert blossoms, the lame leap, sorrow and sighing flee.</p>
      <p class="font-semibold mb-3">Verses worth lingering on</p>
      <ul class="space-y-2.5 text-[15.5px] mb-6">
        <li>• Isaiah 29:13–14 — lips vs. heart; then the marvelous work</li>
        <li>• Isaiah 29:18 — the deaf hear the words of the book</li>
        <li>• Isaiah 30:18 — the Lord waits to be gracious</li>
        <li>• Isaiah 35:1, 10 — the desert blossoms; everlasting joy</li>
      </ul>
      <p class="font-semibold mb-3">Discussion Prompts</p>
      <ul class="space-y-2.5 text-[15.5px]">
        <li>• Why are “marvellous” and “wonder” good words for the Restoration in <em>your</em> life, not just Church history?</li>
        <li>• When have you seen the Lord restore something you thought was gone—faith, a relationship, hope, a habit?</li>
        <li>• Which phrase from Isaiah 35 gives you hope when you need healing?</li>
        <li>• How can we help fulfill these prophecies in Aiea this week—family, neighbors, ministering, temple, inviting?</li>
      </ul>
      <div class="mt-6">
        <p class="font-semibold mb-2">Application Question</p>
        <div class="bg-yellow-50 border-l-4 border-yellow-400 p-5 rounded-xl">
          What is one way you want to take part in the Lord’s “marvellous work” this week?
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
